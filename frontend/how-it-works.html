How AeroPredict works

Plain answers about where our numbers come from, how good they are, and what they can't tell you.

STEP 1

Real records

We collect official on-time records of millions of past flights from governments, airports and air-traffic agencies.

STEP 2

Your flight's history

We look up how your flight number, route, airline and airports have actually performed over the past year.

STEP 3

Your day and time

We adjust for the time of day, the season, holidays and, for flights in the next day or so, the airport weather forecast.

What the numbers mean

For every flight we show three chances that add up to 100%: arriving on time (within 15 minutes of schedule, the standard airlines and regulators use), being delayed by more than 15 minutes, or being cancelled.

These are odds, not promises. A flight with a 20% chance of delay will still be late about one time in five. Things nobody can see in advance, such as a sudden technical fault or a strike, can always change the outcome.

How accurate it is

We test predictions the honest way: on later months of real flights that were kept out of the data the predictions were built from, then compare with what actually happened.

  • US flights: tested on 300,000 flights. A flight that was disrupted gets a higher risk score than one that ran on time about 70% of the time (50% would be guessing), rising to 72% for flights in the next day when the weather forecast is known.
  • Flights elsewhere (tested on Brazil): 30,000 flights, about 67% (up from 54% before route history was added). Of the flights we rated riskiest, predicted at about 40%, 41% were actually delayed or cancelled.

The latest figures are always on the Analytics page.

What we can't do

  • We don't track your plane live. For today's real status, always check your airline or airport.
  • We can't foresee one-off events such as strikes, technical faults or security incidents.
  • Passenger-rights information is a general guide, not legal advice.
